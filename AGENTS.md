# Blog editing

When editing articles, improve the English while preserving Andrei's voice. Use the article being edited as the primary style reference; the blog varies between personal essays and technical tutorials.

## Voice and rhythm

- Keep the personal, conversational voice: first-person experience, direct address, contractions, opinions, admissions of uncertainty, and occasional self-deprecation.
- Preserve dry humour, irony, playful exaggeration, rhetorical questions, parenthetical asides, and purposeful repetition. Do not turn these into formal exposition or automatically answer the rhetorical questions.
- Keep metaphors, cultural references, wordplay, and deliberate ambiguity, even when the wording is unconventional. For example, "unassumed" in the Ormen article is deliberate.
- Retain the variation between long, reflective sentences and short emphatic ones. Do not systematically shorten every sentence or make every paragraph follow the same pattern.
- Preserve the strength of the author's judgments and qualifications. Speculation should remain speculation; do not add certainty, soften opinions, or invent personal experiences.
- Follow each article's existing spelling conventions, terminology, emphasis, and narrative tense, including deliberate historical present.

## Technical articles

- Keep the informal teaching voice alongside precise mathematics and code. Preserve the author's questions, small jokes, and explanations of why a step works.
- When adding requested explanations or solutions, match the surrounding article: concrete examples, visible intermediate steps, and conversational transitions where useful. Avoid generic textbook introductions and repetitive summaries.
- For language-only edits, preserve formulas, code, technical claims, links, section anchors, shortcodes, and interactive markup. Raise substantive technical issues separately.

## Editing boundaries

- Apply unambiguous spelling, grammar, and punctuation corrections directly.
- Ask before changing phrasing or paragraph structure unless the user has explicitly authorised those changes for the current task. Present concrete proposed wording for approval; continue independent grammar fixes while awaiting a reply.
- If an unusual expression has more than one plausible meaning, ask rather than silently choosing one. Do not mistake a distinctive metaphor for a typo.
- Avoid generic editorial polish, promotional language, unnecessary headings, and added conclusions. Do not add jokes, metaphors, or opinions merely to imitate the author.
- Re-read the current file before editing, since the author also edits articles during the conversation. Make targeted changes rather than replacing an article from an old copy.
