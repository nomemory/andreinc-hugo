<div class="mp" id="p00040">
        <p><a class="mpl" href="/olympiad/p00040/">Problem 00040</a></p> 
        <p>Let \(n\ge2\) be an integer, and let \(0 \lt a_1, a_2, \dots, a_n \leq 1\) satisfy \(a_1+a_2+\dots+a_n=n-1\). Prove that:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                (n-1)^n \cdot (1-a_1) \cdot (1-a_2) \cdot \dots \cdot (1-a_n) \leq a_1 a_2 \dots a_n
            \]
        </p>
        <details>   
            <summary>Hint 1</summary>
            <p>This substitution simplifies the inequality into a more recognizable form.</p><p>Try letting \(a_i = 1 - b_i\).</p>
            <p>In this regard, why don't you write \(a_i=1-b_i\)?</p>
        </details>
        <details>
            <summary>Solution</summary>
            <p>We define \(a_i = 1 - b_i\). This is valid because \(a_i \in (0, 1]\) implies \(b_i \in [0, 1)\), and since \(a_i > 0\), we know \(b_i < 1\):</p>
            <p class="mpc">
                \[
                    n-1=\sum_{i=1}^n a_i = \sum_{i=1}^n \left(1-b_i\right) = n - \sum_{i=1}^n b_i \Rightarrow 
                \]
                \[
                    \sum_{i=1}^n b_i = 1
                \]
            </p>
            <p>Substituting back, the desired inequality becomes:</p>
            <p class="mpc">
                \[
                    \left(n-1\right)^n\cdot \prod_{i=1}^n b_i \leq \prod_{i=1}^n \left(1-b_i\right)
                \]
            </p>
            <p>If some \(b_i=0\), then the left-hand side of the transformed inequality is \(0\), so the result is immediate. We may therefore assume that every \(b_i>0\) and divide by \(\prod b_i\):</p>
            <p class="mpc">
                \[
                    \prod_{i=1}^n \left(\frac{1-b_i}{b_i}\right) \geq (n-1)^n
                \]
            </p>
            <p>For each \(i\), use \(1-b_i=\sum_{j\ne i}b_j\) and apply AM-GM to the \(n-1\) terms in this numerator:</p>
            <p class="mpc">
                \[
                    \frac{1-b_i}{b_i}=\frac{\sum_{j\ne i}b_j}{b_i}
                    \ge (n-1)\frac{\left(\prod_{j\ne i}b_j\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}}{b_i}.
                \]
            </p>
            <p>Multiplying these \(n\) inequalities gives:</p>
            <p class="mpc">
                \[
                    \prod_{i=1}^n\frac{1-b_i}{b_i}
                    \ge (n-1)^n
                    \frac{\left(\prod_{i=1}^n\prod_{j\ne i}b_j\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}}{\prod_{i=1}^n b_i}
                    =(n-1)^n,
                \]
            </p>
            <p>because each \(b_j\) occurs exactly \(n-1\) times in the double product. Thus, the inequality is proven. For \(n=2\), equality holds for every admissible pair. For \(n\ge3\), equality holds if and only if \(b_1=\dots=b_n=\frac1n\), or equivalently \(a_1=\dots=a_n=\frac{n-1}{n}\).</p>
        </details>
