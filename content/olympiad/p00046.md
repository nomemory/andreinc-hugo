        <p><a class="mpl" href="/olympiad/p00046/">Problem 00046</a></p> 
        <p>Let \(a,b,c,d\) be positive real numbers and let \(n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge1}\). Prove that:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \frac{a^n+b^n+c^n+d^n}{n} \geq a+b+c+d+2 - 2n
            \]
        </p>
        <details>
            <summary>Hint 1</summary>
            <p>Use the AM-GM inequality by adding \(n-1\) copies of \(1\):</p>
            <p class="mpc">
                \[
                    a^n+\underbrace{1+\dots+1}_{n-1\text{ terms}} \overbrace{\geq}^{\text{AM-GM}} n\cdot a.
                \]
            </p>
        </details>
        <details>
            <summary>Solution</summary>
            <p>If \(n=1\), the claimed inequality is an equality. Suppose now that \(n\ge2\). Applying AM-GM to one copy of \(t^n\) and \(n-1\) copies of \(1\) gives, for every \(t>0\),</p>
            <p class="mpc">
                \[
                    t^n+(n-1)\ge n\sqrt[n]{t^n}=nt.
                \]
            </p>
            <p>Summing this inequality for \(t=a,b,c,d\) gives:</p>
            <p class="mpc">
                \[
                    a^n+b^n+c^n+d^n+4(n-1)\ge n(a+b+c+d).
                \]
            </p>
            <p>Since \(n\ge2\), we have \(4(n-1)\le2n(n-1)\). Hence:</p>
            <p class="mpc">
                \[
                    a^n+b^n+c^n+d^n+2n(n-1)\ge n(a+b+c+d),
                \]
            </p>
            <p>Dividing by \(n\) and rearranging yields exactly the desired inequality. For \(n=1\), equality holds for every positive \(a,b,c,d\). For \(n=2\), equality holds if and only if \(a=b=c=d=1\). For \(n>2\), the comparison \(4(n-1)<2n(n-1)\) is strict, so equality cannot occur.</p>
        </details>
    </div>
