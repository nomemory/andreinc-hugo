<p><a class="mpl" href="/olympiad/p00065/">Problem 00065</a></p> 
<p>Let \(n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\) and let \(x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n\) be positive real numbers. Prove:</p>
<p class="mpc">
\[\sum_{i=1}^n \left[\frac{1}{1+\sum_{j=1}^i x_j}\right] \lt \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n \frac{1}{x_i}} \]
</p>
<details>
    <summary>Solution</summary>
    <p>Let \(s_0=0\) and \(s_k=x_1+x_2+\dots+x_k\) for \(1\leq k\leq n\). We rewrite each term so that the Cauchy-Bunyakovsky-Schwarz inequality can be applied:</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[
            \frac{1}{1+s_k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x_k}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{x_k}}{1+s_k}.
        \]
    </p>
    <p>Therefore:</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[
            \left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1+s_k}\right)^2
            \leq \left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{x_k}\right)
            \left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x_k}{(1+s_k)^2}\right). \tag{1}
        \]
    </p>
    <p>Since \(x_k=s_k-s_{k-1}\) and \(s_k\gt s_{k-1}\), we have:</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[
            \frac{x_k}{(1+s_k)^2}
            \lt \frac{s_k-s_{k-1}}{(1+s_k)(1+s_{k-1})}
            =\frac{1}{1+s_{k-1}}-\frac{1}{1+s_k}.
        \]
    </p>
    <p>Summing this telescoping estimate gives:</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[
            \sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{x_k}{(1+s_k)^2}
            \lt 1-\frac{1}{1+s_n}\lt1. \tag{2}
        \]
    </p>
    <p>Combining \((1)\) and \((2)\), and taking positive square roots, proves the strict inequality.</p>
</details>
