<div class="mp" id="p00103">
<p><a class="mpl" href="/olympiad/p00103/">Problem 00103</a></p> 
<p>Find the positive real numbers \(x,y,z\) satisfying:</p>
<p class="mpc">
    \[
        \begin{cases}
            x^3y + 3 \le 4z \\
            y^3z + 3 \le 4x \\
            z^3x + 3 \le 4y
        \end{cases}
    \]
</p>
<details>
    <summary>Hint 1</summary>
    <p>Try giving values to \(x, y, z\) to find a solution.</p>
</details>
<details>
    <summary>Hint 2</summary>
    <p>Can you prove that:</p> 
    <p class="mpc">
    \[
        x^4y^4z^4\ge(4x-3)(4y-3)(4z-3)
    \]
    </p>
</details>
<details>
    <summary>Hint 3</summary>
    <p>Can you prove that:</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[x^4y^4z^4\le(4x-3)(4y-3)(4z-3)\]
    </p>
</details>
<details>
    <summary>Solution</summary>
    <p>Intuitively, we observe that \(x = y = z = 1\) is a solution. But are there more solutions?</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[
            \begin{cases}
                x^3y + 3 \le 4z \\
                y^3z + 3 \le 4x \\
                z^3x + 3 \le 4y \\
            \end{cases} \Leftrightarrow
            \begin{cases}
                x^3y \le 4z - 3 \\
                y^3z \le 4x - 3 \\
                z^3x \le 4y - 3 \\
            \end{cases}
        \]
    </p>
    <p>The three right-hand sides \(4x-3\), \(4y-3\), and \(4z-3\) are positive because the corresponding left-hand sides are positive. We can therefore multiply all three inequalities to obtain:</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[
            (xyz)^4 \le (4x-3)(4y-3)(4z-3)
        \]
    </p>
    <p>On the other hand, applying the mean inequality chain:</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[
            \begin{cases}
                x^4 + 1 \ge 2x^2 \Rightarrow x^4+3 \ge 2(x^2+1) \ge 4x \Rightarrow x^4 \ge 4x - 3\\
                y^4 + 1 \ge 2y^2 \Rightarrow y^4+3 \ge 2(y^2+1) \ge 4y \Rightarrow y^4 \ge 4y - 3 \\
                z^4 + 1 \ge 2z^2 \Rightarrow z^4+3 \ge 2(z^2+1) \ge 4z \Rightarrow z^4 \ge 4z - 3 \\
            \end{cases}
        \]
    </p>
    <p>Since the three right-hand sides are positive, multiplying the last relationships gives:</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[(xyz)^4 \ge (4x-3)(4y-3)(4z-3)\]
    </p>
    <p>Thus equality holds in the two product estimates. Since each positive ratio \(\frac{x^4}{4x-3}\), \(\frac{y^4}{4y-3}\), and \(\frac{z^4}{4z-3}\) is at least \(1\) and their product is \(1\), all three ratios equal \(1\). Hence equality holds in \(t^4\geq4t-3\) for \(t=x,y,z\).</p>
    <p>Now \(t^4-4t+3=(t-1)^2(t^2+2t+3)\), so a positive \(t\) gives equality only at \(t=1\). Therefore \(x=y=z=1\) is the unique solution.</p>
</details>
