<p><a class="mpl" href="/olympiad/p00107/">Problem 00107</a></p> 
<p>Let \( x,y,z \in (1,\infty) \). Prove that:</p>
<p class="mpc">
\[
    \log_{xy}z+\log_{yz}x+\log_{zx}y\ge\frac{3}{2}
\]
</p>
<details>
    <summary>Hint 1</summary>
    <p>Consider applying a change of base to each logarithmic term. This is a common theme in problems involving logarithms and inequalities.</p>
</details>
<details>
    <summary>Hint 2</summary>
    <p>After the change of base, we have the following:</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[
            \log_{xy} z = \frac{\ln z}{\ln(xy)}
        \]
    </p>
</details>
<details>
    <summary>Solution</summary>
    <p>First, apply the change of base formula to each logarithmic expression:</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[
            \begin{cases}
                \log_{xy}z=\frac{\ln z}{\ln(xy)}=\frac{\ln z}{\ln x+\ln y} \\
                \log_{yz}x=\frac{\ln x}{\ln(yz)}=\frac{\ln x}{\ln y+\ln z} \\
                \log_{zx}y=\frac{\ln y}{\ln(zx)}=\frac{\ln y}{\ln z+\ln x}
            \end{cases}
        \]
    </p>
    <p>Next, substitute \(a=\ln x\), \(b=\ln y\), and \(c=\ln z\). Since \(x,y,z\gt1\), these numbers are positive. This transforms the inequality into:</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[
        \frac{a}{b+c} + \frac{b}{a+c} + \frac{c}{a+b} \ge \frac{3}{2},
        \]
    </p>
    <p>Which is a direct application of Nesbitt's inequality.</p>
    <p>Equality holds if and only if \(x=y=z\).</p>
</details>
