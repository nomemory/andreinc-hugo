<div class="mp" id="p00137">
        <p><a class="mpl" href="/olympiad/p00137/">Problem 00137</a></p> 
        <p>Let \(x,y,z \in (0,1)\) or \(x,y,z \in (1, \infty)\). Prove the following inequality:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \frac{\log_x y}{x+y} + \frac{\log_y z}{y+z} + \frac{\log_z x}{z+x} \geq \frac{9}{2(x+y+z)}
            \]
        </p>
        <details>
            <summary>Hint 1</summary>
            <p>Note that:</p>
            <p class="mpc">
                \[
                    \prod_{\text{cyc}} \log_x y = 1
                \]
            </p>
        </details>
        <details>
            <summary>Solution</summary>
            <p>Set \(A=\log_x y\), \(B=\log_y z\), and \(C=\log_z x\). Under the stated assumptions, \(A,B,C>0\), and \(ABC=1\). We apply Titu's Lemma directly:</p>
            <p class="mpc">
                \[
                    \sum_{\text{cyc}} \frac{\log_x y}{x+y} \geq \frac{\left(\sqrt A+\sqrt B+\sqrt C\right)^2}{2(x+y+z)}.
                \]
            </p>
            <p>By applying the AM-GM inequality to the numerator:</p>
            <p class="mpc">
                \[
                    \sqrt A+\sqrt B+\sqrt C \geq 3\sqrt[3]{\sqrt{ABC}}=3,
                \]
                \[
                    \frac{\left(\sqrt A+\sqrt B+\sqrt C\right)^2}{2(x+y+z)}
                    \geq \frac{9}{2(x+y+z)}.
                \]
            </p>
            <p>Equality holds when \(x=y=z\).</p>
        </details>
        <details>
            <summary>Source</summary>
            <p>Romanian Math Olympiad, Etapa Locala, 10th grade, Arad, 2013</p>
        </details>
