<div class="mp" id="p00160">
    <p><a class="mpl" href="/olympiad/p00160/">Problem 00160</a></p>
    <p>Consider the equation $x^2+(a+b+c)x+k(ab+bc+ca)=0$, where $a,b,c\in\mathbb R_+^*$ and $k\in\mathbb R$. Prove that if $k\le\frac34$, then the equation has real roots.</p>
    <details>
        <summary>Hint</summary>
        <p>Use $(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2\ge0$ to find a lower bound for $(a+b+c)^2$.</p>
    </details>
    <details>
        <summary>Solution</summary>
        <p>For the quadratic to have real roots, its discriminant $\Delta$ must be non-negative:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[ \Delta=(a+b+c)^2-4k(ab+bc+ca). \]
        </p>
        <p>Expanding $(a-b)^2+(b-c)^2+(c-a)^2\ge0$ gives $a^2+b^2+c^2\ge ab+bc+ca$. After adding $2(ab+bc+ca)$, we obtain $(a+b+c)^2\ge3(ab+bc+ca)$. Therefore:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[ \Delta\ge(3-4k)(ab+bc+ca)\ge0, \]
        </p>
        <p>because $k\le\frac34$ and $ab+bc+ca>0$. Hence the equation has real roots.</p>
    </details>
