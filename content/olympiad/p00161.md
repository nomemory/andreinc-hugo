<div class="mp" id="p00161">
    <p><a class="mpl" href="/olympiad/p00161/">Problem 00161</a></p>
    <p>For a real number \(t\), let \(\lfloor t\rfloor\) denote the greatest integer not exceeding \(t\). Prove that, for every \(x\in\mathbb R\),</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[
            \left\lfloor\frac{x+3}{6}\right\rfloor-
            \left\lfloor\frac{x+4}{6}\right\rfloor+
            \left\lfloor\frac{x+5}{6}\right\rfloor
            =\left\lfloor\frac{x+1}{2}\right\rfloor-
            \left\lfloor\frac{x+1}{3}\right\rfloor.
        \]
    </p>
    <details>
        <summary>Hint</summary>
        <p>Set \(y=\frac{x+1}{6}\), then apply Hermite's identity with \(n=3\) and \(n=2\).</p>
    </details>
    <details>
        <summary>Solution</summary>
        <p>Hermite's identity states that, for every real \(y\) and positive integer \(n\),</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left\lfloor y+\frac{j}{n}\right\rfloor=\lfloor ny\rfloor.
            \]
        </p>
        <p>Set \(y=\frac{x+1}{6}\). Then</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \begin{aligned}
                \frac{x+3}{6}&=y+\frac13,&
                \frac{x+4}{6}&=y+\frac12,&
                \frac{x+5}{6}&=y+\frac23,\\
                \frac{x+1}{2}&=3y,&
                \frac{x+1}{3}&=2y.
                \end{aligned}
            \]
        </p>
        <p>Thus the identity to be proved is equivalent to</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \left\lfloor y+\frac13\right\rfloor-
                \left\lfloor y+\frac12\right\rfloor+
                \left\lfloor y+\frac23\right\rfloor
                =\lfloor3y\rfloor-\lfloor2y\rfloor. \tag{1}
            \]
        </p>
        <p>Hermite's identity with \(n=3\) and \(n=2\), respectively, gives</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \begin{aligned}
                \lfloor3y\rfloor
                &=\lfloor y\rfloor+\left\lfloor y+\frac13\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor y+\frac23\right\rfloor,\\
                \lfloor2y\rfloor
                &=\lfloor y\rfloor+\left\lfloor y+\frac12\right\rfloor.
                \end{aligned}
            \]
        </p>
        <p>Subtracting the second equality from the first cancels \(\lfloor y\rfloor\) and gives exactly (1). This proves the original identity for every real \(x\).</p>
    </details>
