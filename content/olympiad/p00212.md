<div class="mp" id="p00212">
    <p><a class="mpl" href="/olympiad/p00212/">Problem 00212</a></p>
    <p>Let \(n\ge1\) be an integer and let \(a_1,a_2,\ldots,a_n\ge0\) be real numbers such that:</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[
            \sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i^2=n.
        \]
    </p>
    <p>Prove that, for every integer \(m\ge1\),</p>
    <p class="mpc">
        \[
            \sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_i^{2m+1}}{\sqrt{a_i+(n-1)a_{i+1}}}
            \ge
            \frac{n}{\sqrt{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i}},
        \]
    </p>
    <p>where the indices are taken cyclically, so \(a_{n+1}=a_1\). A term with a zero denominator is understood by continuity.</p>
    <details>
        <summary>Hint 1</summary>
        <p>Rewrite each summand in Engel form so that the Cauchy-Bunyakovsky-Schwarz inequality applies:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \frac{a_i^{2m+1}}{\sqrt{a_i+(n-1)a_{i+1}}}
                =
                \frac{\left(a_i^{m+1}\right)^2}{a_i\sqrt{a_i+(n-1)a_{i+1}}}.
            \]
        </p>
    </details>
    <details>
        <summary>Hint 2</summary>
        <p>Write \(S_k=\sum_i a_i^k\). Repeated CBS applications show that \(S_k^2\le S_{k-1}S_{k+1}\) for \(k\ge2\), so the ratios \(S_{k+1}/S_k\) are nondecreasing from that point onward. Use this to bound \(S_{m+1}\), then apply CBS to:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i\sqrt{a_i+(n-1)a_{i+1}}.
            \]
        </p>
    </details>
    <details>
        <summary>Solution</summary>
        <p>For indices with \(a_i=0\), the corresponding summand is \(0\) by continuity, so those terms may be omitted. Applying the Cauchy-Bunyakovsky-Schwarz inequality in Engel form, we obtain:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \begin{aligned}
                \sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_i^{2m+1}}{\sqrt{a_i+(n-1)a_{i+1}}}
                &=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\left(a_i^{m+1}\right)^2}{a_i\sqrt{a_i+(n-1)a_{i+1}}}\\
                &\overbrace{\ge}^{\text{CBS}}
                \frac{\left(\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i^{m+1}\right)^2}
                {\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i\sqrt{a_i+(n-1)a_{i+1}}}.
                \end{aligned}
            \]
        </p>
        <p>We next bound the numerator using CBS alone. Put \(S_k=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i^k\). First,</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                S_1^2=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i\right)^2
                \overbrace{\le}^{\text{CBS}}n\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i^2=n^2,
            \]
        </p>
        <p>so \(S_1\le n=S_2\). Moreover, for every \(k\ge2\), another application of CBS gives</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                S_k^2
                =\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i^{(k-1)/2}a_i^{(k+1)/2}\right)^2
                \overbrace{\le}^{\text{CBS}}S_{k-1}S_{k+1}.
            \]
        </p>
        <p>All \(S_k\) with \(k\ge1\) are positive because \(S_2=n>0\). Hence the ratios \(S_{k+1}/S_k\) are nondecreasing. Since \(S_2/S_1\ge1\), it follows successively that \(S_3\ge S_2\), \(S_4\ge S_3\), and so on. Thus \(S_{m+1}\ge S_2=n\), and therefore:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_i^{2m+1}}{\sqrt{a_i+(n-1)a_{i+1}}}
                \ge
                \frac{n^2}{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i\sqrt{a_i+(n-1)a_{i+1}}}.
                \tag{1}
            \]
        </p>
        <p>We now estimate the denominator using the Cauchy-Bunyakovsky-Schwarz inequality:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \begin{aligned}
                \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i\sqrt{a_i+(n-1)a_{i+1}}\right)^2
                &=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sqrt{a_i}\sqrt{a_i^2+(n-1)a_ia_{i+1}}\right)^2\\
                &\overbrace{\le}^{\text{CBS}}
                \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i\right)
                \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i^2+(n-1)\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_ia_{i+1}\right).
                \end{aligned}
            \]
        </p>
        <p>Because the indices are cyclic,</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \sum_{i=1}^{n}a_ia_{i+1}
                \le\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(a_i^2+a_{i+1}^2\right)
                =\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i^2=n.
            \]
        </p>
        <p>It follows that:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i\sqrt{a_i+(n-1)a_{i+1}}\right)^2
                \le n^2\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i,
            \]
        </p>
        <p>and hence:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i\sqrt{a_i+(n-1)a_{i+1}}
                \le n\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i}.
                \tag{2}
            \]
        </p>
        <p>Combining (1) and (2), we conclude that:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_i^{2m+1}}{\sqrt{a_i+(n-1)a_{i+1}}}
                \ge
                \frac{n^2}{n\sqrt{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i}}
                =
                \frac{n}{\sqrt{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i}}.
            \]
        </p>
        <p>Equality holds for \(a_1=a_2=\cdots=a_n=1\).</p>
    </details>
