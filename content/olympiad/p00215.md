<div class="mp" id="p00215">
    <p><a class="mpl" href="/olympiad/p00215/">Problem 00215</a></p>
    <p>Prove that \(\log_{10}21\) is irrational.</p>
    <details>
        <summary>Solution</summary>
        <p>Suppose, for a contradiction, that \(\log_{10}21\) is rational. Since it is positive, we can write it as \(\frac{p}{q}\), where \(p\) and \(q\) are positive integers. Then:</p>
        <p class="mpc">
            \[
                \log_{10}21 = \frac{p}{q}
                \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                21 = 10^{\frac{p}{q}}
                \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
                21^q = 10^p.
            \]
        </p>
        <p>But this is impossible: \(21^q\) always ends in \(1\), while \(10^p\) always ends in \(0\). So no positive integers \(p\) and \(q\) can satisfy this equality, and \(\log_{10}21\) is irrational.</p>
    </details>

        <summary>Source</summary>

    </details>
